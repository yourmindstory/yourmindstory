What does change actually look like?

Not forgetting him overnight. Not never thinking about him. Not suddenly not caring.

Change can look like the loop taking less space. Sleeping. Feeling calmer when the urge arrives. Following through. Feeling more like yourself.

These are women's own words about their experiences.

Kimberly

“I still have the loop but it's not consuming me constantly.”

What matters here is not that the thoughts magically disappeared. Kimberly described the loop taking up less of her mind.

Jessica

“I've managed 8 days no contact whilst still living under the same roof so I'm really proud of myself x”

“I honestly listen to your audio so frequently it really puts me right back where I need to be!”

She was beginning to do something she wanted to do in a difficult situation.

Sometimes the first change is simply getting through the night.

“For the first time in 5 weeks I was able to regulate my breathing and stop my racing heart and I actually slept.”
CeeJay

Sometimes change begins with getting some space from what has been consuming you.

Returning to yourself can mean different things.

“I'm slowly unraveling from years of survival mode. I'm finding my center and power again.”

“I brought these meditations to help me deal with childhood cptsd and abandonment issues, which means I can suffer with limerance and end up tolerating abusive dynamics. I am really pleased I bought it and will be purchasing again.”

There isn't one version of progress.

The more useful questions are: what happens when the thought comes? Does it take the whole day? Does impulse automatically become action? Can you increasingly follow your own decisions? Does your attention start returning to your own life?

You do not need a miracle story. You need a sensible place to start.
