You probably don’t need more information about him.

You’ve watched the videos. Read the posts. Learned about avoidant attachment.

Maybe you can explain why he pulls away. Maybe you understand the hot and cold better than you ever wanted to.

And you still find yourself staring at your phone.

Still replaying what he said.

Still wondering what changed.

Still knowing you shouldn’t check and checking anyway.

Still making a decision and then questioning it the minute he comes back, goes quiet, or something inside you gets triggered.

More information can help you understand why this is happening. But information alone does not automatically change what happens in you when it happens again.

So at some point the question needs to change.

Not: Why is he doing this?

But: What has this started doing to me?

Because the cost is not only the two minutes you spend checking his profile.

It’s what happens afterwards.

The hour you lose replaying. The sleep. The plan you cancel. The conversation you’re only half present for. The decision you made yesterday that suddenly feels impossible to trust today.

The amount of your life that starts happening around him, the situation, or the aftermath of it.

Even when he isn’t physically there. Even when you’re on a break. Even when you’re in no contact. Even when he is silent. Even when the relationship has ended.

It can still feel like he is in your head all the time.

That is the part we need to work on.

Why four steps?

Something happens and your body sounds the alarm.

It’s automatic.

That alarm creates the impulse to check, reach out, replay, investigate, ask questions or get reassurance. The list goes on.

When that keeps happening, a second problem starts to appear.

You stop trusting yourself.

You say you won’t call, then you call.

You decide you’re done, then question yourself.

You don’t know what was true and what wasn’t.

You wonder whether you were too needy, too demanding, too sensitive, or whether you imagined the whole thing.

And eventually so much of your attention can be going towards him, what happened, what might happen, or what it all meant that you start losing contact with yourself.

You don’t even have to still be in the relationship for that to be happening.

Step 1

Quiet the Alarm

This is the part that can happen before you have even had time to think.

No message. A different tone. Seeing him online. Remembering something. Thinking about what he might be doing. Something just feels off.

Your chest tightens. Your stomach drops. Your breathing changes. Your mind starts running. You need to know what is happening.

We don’t start by telling you to stop caring or to “just don’t check.” We start by working on the automatic response that is making everything feel urgent in the first place.

A message, a silence, a thought or a memory does not have to send your whole system into alert. You can think, sleep and get through your day without everything suddenly feeling like an emergency.

Step 2

Break the Pull

You can feel completely clear one minute.

Then the urge hits.

Check. Message him. Read the conversation again. Look at his social media. See whether he is online. Work out what he meant. Ask another question. Find one more piece of information that might finally make you feel better.

Sometimes you have checked before you have even really decided to.

You already know the relief rarely lasts. Then the urge comes back.

So this step is not about spending three weeks arguing with yourself. We work on the pull underneath the behaviour.

You can feel the urge without automatically checking, reaching out or going back. The feeling can be there without making the decision for you.

Step 3

Restore Self-Trust

At some point this can stop being only about whether you trust him.

It becomes about whether you trust yourself.

You notice something isn’t right, then wonder if you’re overreacting.

You decide what you need, then talk yourself out of it.

You set a boundary, then worry that you asked for too much.

You say you won’t call, then you call. You say you’re done, then the feeling changes and suddenly you don’t know anymore.

You start questioning what was real, what wasn’t, and whether you can trust your own judgement at all.

You start believing yourself again. You can make a decision, hold a boundary and hear your own judgement without having to reopen everything the moment your feelings change or he comes back.

Step 4

Return to Yourself

This is bigger than thinking about him less.

It’s about your life becoming yours again.

Your attention. Your decisions. Your time. Your peace. Your relationships. Your plans. The things you care about.

The thoughts, memories, waiting, questions and effects of what happened stop being the thing your life has to keep orbiting around.

Your attention starts belonging to your own life again. You can make plans, be present with people you love and have mental space that is not occupied by him.

What does “take your life back” actually mean?

It does not mean you become somebody who never thinks about him again.

It does not mean you stop loving him on command.

And it does not mean becoming a woman who needs nobody.

It can start much more simply.

He goes quiet and your entire day doesn’t disappear with him.

You see him online and you don’t need to investigate what it means.

You feel the urge to check and don’t automatically act on it.

You miss him without abandoning yourself.

He comes back and the relief does not have to make the decision for you.

You set a boundary and don’t spend the rest of the evening wondering whether you were unreasonable.

You make a decision and don’t have to remake it every time your feelings change.

You are with somebody you love and you are actually there.

You stop needing to know what he is thinking before you are allowed to have peace.

And this is where you start fighting for yourself like nobody has ever fought for you.

Every time you press play.

Every time you keep the promise you made to yourself.

Every time you feel the urge and choose not to abandon yourself just to make the feeling stop.

You are teaching yourself something at the automatic level where this pattern has been playing out:

I am here. I will not abandon myself to keep connection with somebody else.

And the more you demonstrate that to yourself, the less willing you become to repeatedly abandon yourself for somebody who cannot or will not meet you with the same care, consistency or capacity.

You become someone you can trust.

Why Cognitive Behavioural Hypnotherapy?

Because the frustrating part is often that you already know.

You know checking probably won’t give you what you want.

You know another message won’t create certainty.

You know replaying what happened for the hundredth time is unlikely to reveal the answer.

And knowing that does not necessarily stop the response when it hits.

Cognitive Behavioural Hypnotherapy gives us a way to work with thoughts, attention, behaviour and learned responses rather than only adding more information.

That is why the Bootcamp is not another 12 weeks of learning more about avoidant men. We have enough to work on already: what is happening in you.

Questions you might still have

What if I’m still with him?

You can still do the Bootcamp. You do not have to stop loving somebody before you can stop losing yourself in the relationship.

What if it’s already over?

Ending the relationship and ending what is happening inside you are not always the same thing. If you are still checking, replaying, waiting, hoping, questioning yourself or spending huge amounts of time thinking about him, there is still something here to work on.

Are you going to tell me to go no contact?

No. This programme is not here to make your relationship decisions for you. It is here to help you get steadier, clearer and more connected to yourself while you make them.

I already know loads about avoidant attachment. Is this going to tell me everything I already know?

No. If information alone were enough, you probably would not still be fighting the same internal battle. This is not the next piece of information. It is the next piece of work.

I’ve already tried therapy. Why would this be different?

This is not a claim that therapy failed or that the Bootcamp replaces therapy. This programme is deliberately focused on this specific pattern and on practising different responses through guided Cognitive Behavioural Hypnotherapy, weekly reflection and progress check-ins. The question is not whether you have understood yourself before. It is whether this particular pattern is still running your thoughts, reactions and choices now.

What if I’m the kind of person who buys a course and then doesn’t finish it?

That is exactly why I have not built this like a heavy online course. Your main daily job is to press play. Once a week you reflect and check in. Every three weeks we look at what is changing. If you miss a day or have a bad week, the instruction is not “you failed.” It is return.

What if I check, contact him or get pulled back in during the 12 weeks?

That does not automatically mean the work is not working and it does not mean you have failed the programme. We use what happened as information. Notice it, check in, return to the work and keep building the response you want to have more often.

I don’t have loads of spare time. How much am I actually being asked to do?

Your main daily job is the guided work. Once a week, allow around 15 minutes for your Your Mind Story reflection and short check-in. The group integration calls happen every three weeks. This is deliberately not built around weekly teaching videos, piles of worksheets or daily journalling.

Why wouldn’t I just buy one of the £37 audios?

If one part of the cycle is clearly the problem, a focused audio may be enough to start. I would not push you into the Bootcamp just because it exists. The Bootcamp is for when several parts are feeding each other, or when you want to work through the whole pattern in order: the alarm, the pull, your self-trust and getting your attention and life back to you.

What if I’ve been stuck in this for years?

You do not have to be newly out of the relationship for this to be relevant. We start from where you are now and track your movement against your own baseline. The aim is not to pretend years disappear overnight. It is to work on the reactions and patterns that are still active in your life now.

What if I miss one of the live calls?

A missed call does not erase the work you have already done. The core work continues through your current guided step, weekly reflection and check-in. The calls are integration points in the programme, not the only place the work happens. Return to your current step and keep going.
