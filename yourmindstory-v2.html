It's Not Him. It's The Hook.

Why your brain won't let go.

So why can't you stop thinking about him?

Even when you know better. Even when you've told yourself to stop. Even when you know the dynamic is draining you.

It's not love.

It's not weakness.

It is intermittent reinforcement - one of the most powerful neurological conditioning mechanisms that exists.

Closeness... then distance.

Warmth... then withdrawal.

Your brain doesn't just process that. It locks in. It begins functioning like a slot machine - constantly scanning and replaying, trying to get back to how it felt at the start.

In March 2026 a Los Angeles jury confirmed what you have been living - that intermittent reinforcement overrides human agency. It is legally classified as a defective design.

You weren't obsessed. You were conditioned. That is not a character flaw.

Why thinking your way out isn't working

Because this isn't a thinking problem.

You didn't get conditioned into this through logic. So you cannot get out of it through logic. This is why you can understand avoidant attachment perfectly - and still feel that pull every single morning.

Understanding the pattern is one layer.

Interrupting the loop is another.

Patterns like this change through repetition, not willpower. You cannot talk your nervous system out of a biological hook. Knowing why you are in the spiral doesn't stop the spiral from spinning.

Why you didn't see it coming

Because individuals with high avoidance often present as secure during the first 90 days. Once the threat of real intimacy triggers their defence system, they shut down - becoming effectively unreachable to the person they claimed to love.

There is nothing you could have done to change this.

What is often labelled anxious attachment is actually reactive anxiety. It is your nervous system's sane, protective response to a partner who suddenly became unsafe. You are trying to troubleshoot a system failure you didn't create.
