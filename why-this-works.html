Why This Works

You understand the pattern.
So why hasn't understanding it stopped your response to it?

Understanding the pattern is not the same as changing your response to the pattern.

That gap is important.

Knowing what you want to do and doing it in the moment are different skills.

You can know you do not need to check. Then something triggers you. A thought. A memory. Silence. A feeling that something might have changed.

Suddenly knowing what you want to do is competing with what you feel overwhelmingly pulled to do.

The goal is not simply for you to become better at explaining why you check.

We want to practise a different response when the urge actually appears.

“I want to check” does not automatically have to become “I'm checking.”

What Cognitive Behavioral Hypnotherapy means here

Cognitive

Working with thoughts, interpretations and beliefs.

Behavioral

Practising what you want to do differently when the response appears.

Hypnosis

Using focused attention, imagery and therapeutic suggestion as part of that practice.

You participate

You are not asleep. You have not handed your mind over to me. You are participating in the process.

We're not just analysing the response. We're practising another response.

This is not positive thinking, pretending you do not care, or forcing yourself to believe something you do not believe.

What does the research say?

There is legitimate research on clinical hypnosis and on hypnosis used alongside cognitive behavioral approaches. An updated 2021 meta-analysis combined 48 post-treatment comparisons involving 1,928 participants and found small-to-medium statistically significant advantages for CBT enhanced with hypnosis overall at post-treatment, with effects varying by outcome.

That evidence is broader than Your Mind Story and does not mean every person or every problem responds in the same way. It does mean hypnosis alongside cognitive behavioral approaches is a legitimate area of clinical research rather than a claim that depends on exaggerated neuroscience.

Research reference: Ramondo N, Gignac GE, Pestell CF, Byrne SM. Clinical Hypnosis as an Adjunct to Cognitive Behavior Therapy: An Updated Meta-Analysis. International Journal of Clinical and Experimental Hypnosis. 2021;69(2):169-202. PMID 33646087.

So what does success actually look like?

Not never thinking about him again.

It can look like a trigger disrupting less of your day. Experiencing an urge without automatically checking. Making a decision and increasingly trusting yourself to follow through. Spending less time analysing him and more time participating in your own life.

The goal isn't to become a woman who feels nothing. It's to become a woman whose life is no longer organised around what somebody else is or isn't doing.

You do not have to be over him or decide what happens with the relationship before you can work on what this is doing to you.
